% ── Abstract ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
We present an embodied autonomous bronchoscope platform that integrates
multimodal perception with goal-conditioned imitation learning for
autonomous airway navigation and mucus clearance.
The system fuses real-time semantic segmentation (UNet), metric depth
estimation (Depth-Anything-V2, ViT-L encoder), and depth-guided
bifurcation analysis (DepthPathFinder) into a unified
20-dimensional structured observation vector that captures lumen
geometry, path topology, and instrument proprioception.
A goal-conditioned gated recurrent network (BronchusPolicy) maps this
multimodal observation sequence and a discrete anatomical target
identifier to three-degree-of-freedom motor increments, enabling
voice-commanded navigation to any of nine anatomical destinations.
Expert demonstrations collected at 20\,Hz in HDF5 format train the
policy via behavior cloning with an auxiliary subtask-classification
head that improves action quality across both navigation and
mucus-clearance phases.
Experiments on a bronchial phantom demonstrate a navigation success
rate of \TODO{XX\%}, a mean endpoint error of \TODO{XX\,mm}, and
end-to-end inference at 20\,Hz.
The integrated platform—spanning hardware, perception, policy, and a
voice-driven user interface—provides a clinically deployable foundation
for fully autonomous bronchoscopy.
\end{abstract}

% ── I. Introduction ─────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Flexible bronchoscopy is essential for airway inspection, biopsy, and
mucus clearance in patients with respiratory diseases.
Skilled navigation through the highly branched tracheobronchial tree
demands years of training to coordinate real-time endoscopic video with
instrument tip deflection, and operator fatigue remains a safety
concern during repeated bronchial toilet in intensive care.

Robotic bronchoscopy has attracted considerable attention, with
commercial systems (e.g., Intuitive Ion, Auris Monarch) providing
teleoperation assistance~\cite{swaney2017toward}.
Yet none currently offer fully autonomous waypoint navigation:
the operator must still select each bifurcation turn manually.
Key technical barriers include: (i)~robust lumen perception in the
textureless, specular, and wet endoscopic environment;
(ii)~bifurcation-aware path selection under anatomical variability;
and (iii)~learning policies that generalize without explicit kinematic
models of the flexible instrument.

Recent vision foundation models have dramatically advanced per-frame
depth estimation~\cite{yang2024depthv2} and semantic parsing,
while recurrent imitation-learning architectures have enabled
end-to-end visuomotor policies in
manipulation~\cite{chi2023diffusion,zhao2023act} and flexible
endoscopy~\cite{liu2023bronchoscopy}.
Building on these threads, we present a unified platform in which
every subsystem—hardware actuation, multimodal perception, learned
policy, and human-robot interaction—is integrated and co-designed
for 20\,Hz closed-loop bronchoscopy.

The main contributions of this letter are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A \textbf{multimodal perception pipeline} that fuses UNet lumen
        segmentation, Depth-Anything-V2 metric depth, and a
        depth-region bifurcation analyser (DepthPathFinder) into a
        compact 20-dimensional observation space.
  \item A \textbf{goal-conditioned GRU policy} (BronchusPolicy) with a
        goal-embedding mechanism that supports directed navigation to
        any of nine bronchial anatomical sites via a single learned
        network.
  \item A \textbf{dual-head training objective} that jointly supervises
        motor-increment regression and subtask classification
        (navigate vs.\ clear-mucus), improving action quality in both
        operating phases.
  \item A \textbf{fully integrated system} with voice-command interaction
        and an expert-data collection pipeline, validated on
        \TODO{phantom / ex-vivo porcine lung}.
\end{itemize}

% ── II. Related Work ────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Robotic Bronchoscopy}
Tendon-driven continuum robots provide the mechanical basis for
flexible bronchoscopy~\cite{camarillo2008mechanics,roesthuis2014continuum}.
Shape-sensing and model-based controllers have been proposed for
bronchial navigation, but they require accurate instrument models and
pre-operative CT registration that are difficult to maintain under
tissue deformation~\cite{swaney2017toward}.

\subsection{Endoscope Visual Perception}
Monocular depth estimation in endoscopy is challenging due to
specular highlights and the absence of texture.
Self-supervised approaches have been explored~\cite{wu2023depth},
but metric accuracy remains limited without fine-tuning on
domain-specific data.
Depth-Anything-V2~\cite{yang2024depthv2} achieves state-of-the-art
metric depth on diverse scenes with a ViT-L backbone, which we
adapt here to the bronchoscopic domain.

\subsection{Autonomous Navigation via Imitation Learning}
Behavior cloning (BC) trains a policy to mimic expert demonstrations
directly~\cite{ross2011dagger}.
Recurrent architectures such as GRU~\cite{cho2014gru} handle
partial observability in sequential navigation tasks.
Recent work on autonomous colonoscopy and flexible endoscope
control~\cite{jiang2023endoscope} demonstrates the feasibility of
learning-based approaches, but has not addressed goal-directed
navigation across the full anatomical hierarchy of the bronchial tree.
Goal conditioning via discrete
embeddings~\cite{chi2023diffusion,zhao2023act} has proven effective
in manipulation; we transfer this idea to bronchoscopy for the first
time.

Fig.~\ref{fig:system} shows the system architecture.
A finite-state machine (FSM) manages four operation modes:
\textit{Manual}~(M1) joystick teleoperation,
\textit{Vision}~(M2) reactive visual servoing,

A dedicated camera thread runs the perception pipeline;
a separate control thread runs the FSM and policy inference.
Thread-safe feature dictionaries synchronize the two threads without
blocking.

\subsection{Robotic Bronchoscope}

The prototype retrofits a commercial flexible bronchoscope with a
four-way deflectable tip, driven by three brushless RMD motors:

\label{tab:motors}
\centering
\begin{tabular}{clcc}
\toprule
DoF & Function & Range (deg) & Gearbox \\
\midrule
M0 & Advancement / withdrawal  & $[-900,\ 0]$   & 1:1  \\
M1 & Left–right tip deflection & $[-170,\ 170]$ & 1:1  \\
M2 & Up–down tip deflection    & $[-500,\ 500]$ & 10:1 \\
\bottomrule

Motors are commanded over a UART serial bus (1\,Mbit/s) as position
targets with velocity limits.
The 10:1 reduction gearbox on M2 reduces effective output range to
$\pm$50$^\circ$ while increasing torque for stiff tissue contact.
The closed-loop control cycle runs at \textbf{20\,Hz}.

\subsection{Sensor Suite}

An endoscopic USB camera captures 640$\times$480 frames at 30\,fps.
A circular region of interest (ROI) of 788\,px diameter is extracted
and barrel-distortion-corrected using precomputed maps stored as a
compressed \texttt{.npz} file.
An Xbox controller provides teleoperation input for the Manual and
Hybrid modes, and a microphone feeds the Baidu cloud automatic speech
recognition (ASR) service for Chinese-language voice commands.

% ── V. Multimodal Perception ────────────────────────────────────
\section{Multimodal Perception}
\label{sec:perception}

\subsection{Semantic Segmentation (UNet)}

A UNet~\cite{ronneberger2015unet} with a VGG-16
backbone~\cite{simonyan2014vgg}, fine-tuned on \TODO{$N$} annotated
bronchoscopic frames, performs four-class per-pixel classification:
\textit{background}, \textit{lumen}, \textit{bifurcation}, and
\textit{mucus/stone}.
Inference runs at $\approx$30\,fps on a consumer GPU.
From the predicted probability maps we extract:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Mucus centroid} $(c_x, c_y)$: pixel centroid of the
        mucus/stone class, normalized to $[-1,1]$ within the ROI;
        binary detection flag; and area ratio $s_\text{stone}$.
  \item \textbf{Bifurcation centroid} $(b_x, b_y)$ and area ratio
        $s_\text{bif}$: analogous quantities for the bifurcation class.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Metric Depth Estimation (Depth-Anything-V2)}

Depth-Anything-V2~\cite{yang2024depthv2} with a ViT-L encoder and a
metric-depth head (fine-tuned on the Hypersim dataset) estimates
per-pixel absolute depth in millimeters from the barrel-corrected ROI
(resized to 518$\times$518).
We apply:
(i)~a validity gate $[Z_\text{min}, Z_\text{max}] = [5, 300]$\,mm;
(ii)~a temporal jump filter that discards frames in which the mean
depth changes by more than 30\,mm from the previous frame.

Three scalar depth statistics are computed from the valid depth map:
\begin{equation}
  d_\text{ctr},\; d_\text{mean},\; d_\text{max}
  \label{eq:depth_stats}
\end{equation}
corresponding to the image-center depth, the lumen-masked mean depth,
and the 95th-percentile (``deepest navigable'') depth, respectively.

\subsection{DepthPathFinder: Bifurcation and Path Analysis}

At bifurcation zones, the depth map is partitioned into a
$3\!\times\!5$ spatial grid.
Each of three columns (left, center, right) accumulates depth
statistics over the distal half of the lumen mask, yielding path-depth
estimates $p_L$, $p_C$, $p_R$ (in mm).
These are passed directly to the observation vector (Section~\ref{sec:obs}),
enabling the policy to reason about available paths without an
explicit 3-D map or pre-operative CT registration.

\subsection{Structured Observation Vector}
\label{sec:obs}

All perception outputs and motor proprioception are assembled into a
\textbf{20-dimensional} structured observation vector $\obs_t$:

\begin{equation}
  \obs_t = \bigl[
    \underbrace{c_x,\,c_y,\,s_\text{stone},\,\delta_\text{stone},\,
                b_x,\,b_y,\,s_\text{bif}}_{\text{visual (7)}},\;
    \underbrace{d_\text{ctr},\,d_\text{mean},\,d_\text{max},\,
                p_L,\,p_C,\,p_R}_{\text{depth (6)}},\;
    \underbrace{m_0,\,m_1,\,m_2}_{\text{pos (3)}},\;
    \underbrace{\dot m_0,\,\dot m_1,\,\dot m_2}_{\text{vel (3)}},\;
    \underbrace{\Delta t}_{\text{dt (1)}}
  \bigr]^\top
  \label{eq:obs}
\end{equation}

Each field is normalized by a fixed scale factor (Table~\ref{tab:obs_fields}).
Velocity $\dot{m}_i$ is estimated as the finite difference of consecutive
angle readings divided by $\Delta t$.

\section{Goal-Conditioned Policy Learning}

\subsection{Policy Architecture}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig3_network.pdf}
  \caption{BronchusPolicy architecture.
           Observations $\obs_{t-T+1:\,t}$ are encoded frame-wise
           by an MLP encoder and concatenated with a goal embedding
           $\mathbf{e}_\goal$ before being processed by a two-layer GRU.
           The final hidden state produces motor-increment predictions
           (action head) and subtask classification (task head).
           \TODO{Replace with actual figure.}}
  \label{fig:network}
\end{figure}

Given a sliding window of $\Tobs=8$ consecutive observations and a
discrete goal token $\goal \in \{0,\ldots,9\}$ (Table~\ref{tab:goals}),
BronchusPolicy (Fig.~\ref{fig:network}) outputs:
(1)~a normalized action vector $\act_t\!\in\![-1,1]^3$ representing motor
increments $[\Delta m_0, \Delta m_1, \Delta m_2]$; and
(2)~subtask logits $\mathbf{z}_t\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^2$ (navigate~/ clear).

\begin{table}[h]
\caption{Anatomical Goal Tokens}
\label{tab:goals}
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{clcl}
\toprule
$\goal$ & Target & $\goal$ & Target \\
\midrule
0 & Autonomous exploration & 5 & Left lower lobe (LLB) \\
1 & Trachea (TR)           & 6 & Right upper lobe (RUL) \\
2 & L. main bronchus (LMB) & 7 & Bronchus intermedius (BI) \\
3 & R. main bronchus (RMB) & 8 & Right middle lobe (RML) \\
4 & Left upper lobe (LUB)  & 9 & Right lower lobe (RLL) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The network comprises three learnable modules:

\textbf{ObsEncoder.}
A two-layer MLP ($\dobs\!\to\!128\!\to\!128$, LayerNorm + ELU) encodes
each frame independently:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{e}^{\text{obs}}_t = \text{ObsEncoder}(\obs_t).
\end{equation}

\textbf{Goal embedding.}
An embedding table $\mathbf{W}_g \in \mathbb{R}^{\Ngoal \times 16}$
maps the integer goal token to a 16-dim vector that is broadcast to
all $\Tobs$ time steps:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{e}_\goal = \text{Embed}(\goal);\quad
  \mathbf{u}_t = [\mathbf{e}^{\text{obs}}_t \,\|\, \mathbf{e}_\goal].
  \label{eq:goal_emb}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Temporal GRU.}
A two-layer GRU (hidden size 256, inter-layer dropout 0.1) processes
the concatenated sequence $\{\mathbf{u}_t\}_{t=1}^\Tobs \in \mathbb{R}^{144}$:
\begin{equation}
  \hid_t = \text{GRU}(\mathbf{u}_t,\, \hid_{t-1}),
  \label{eq:gru}
\end{equation}
and the final hidden state $\hid_T$ feeds two prediction heads:

\begin{align}
  \act_t &= \tanh\!\bigl(\text{MLP}_\text{act}(\hid_T)\bigr),
            \quad \act_t\!\in\![-1,1]^3 \label{eq:action_head}\\
  \mathbf{z}_t &= \text{MLP}_\text{task}(\hid_T),
            \quad \mathbf{z}_t\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^2.   \label{eq:task_head}
\end{align}

Action increments are de-normalized as $[\Delta m_0, \Delta m_1, \Delta m_2]
= \act_t \odot [5, 3, 3]$\,deg/frame before clamping to motor limits.
Total trainable parameters: $\approx\!0.4\,$M.
Orthogonal weight initialization~\cite{zhao2023act} is applied throughout.

\subsection{Expert Demonstration Collection}

Demonstrations are recorded during manual teleoperation using

Each frame stores the full 20-dim observation vector, a motor-angle
triplet $(m_0, m_1, m_2)$, a task label $y\!\in\!\{0,1\}$
(navigate / clear-mucus), and a path label $\goal$.

\subsection{Behavior Cloning Training}

The training objective combines MSE action regression and
cross-entropy subtask classification:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = \underbrace{\|\act_t - \hat{\act}_t\|_2^2}_{\mathcal{L}_\text{action}}
              + \lambda\,\underbrace{H(\mathbf{z}_t,\, y_t)}_{\mathcal{L}_\text{task}},
  \quad \lambda = 0.3.
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}

Training uses AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2017adamw} with initial
learning rate $3\!\times\!10^{-4}$, cosine annealing, batch size 64,
sequence length $\Tobs\!=\!8$, mixed-precision (AMP), and early stopping
with patience 30.
A 90/10 train/validation split is used.
Gradients are clipped to norm 1.0.

\subsection{Real-Time Inference}

At runtime, \texttt{BCRunner} maintains a rolling FIFO buffer of $\Tobs$
frames.
Each control tick:
(1)~the perception thread writes fresh features to a thread-safe
dictionary;
(2)~the buffer is updated and stacked into a $[1, \Tobs, 20]$ tensor;
(3)~a forward pass through BronchusPolicy yields $\act_t$ and $\mathbf{z}_t$;
(4)~scaled motor increments are applied as position-control commands.
Measured wall-clock inference latency is $<$5\,ms on an NVIDIA RTX 3090,
sustaining the 20\,Hz control loop.

% ── VIII. Discussion ────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Discussion}

\textbf{Goal conditioning.}
Results in Table~\ref{tab:ablation} confirm that the goal embedding
provides a substantial benefit over exploration-mode navigation,
since the policy can learn target-specific entry angles at each
bifurcation.

\textbf{Dual-head training.}
The auxiliary subtask-classification loss encourages the GRU to
maintain a distinct internal representation of the navigation vs.\
mucus-clearance regimes, which correlates with lower action MSE on
both task types.

\textbf{Limitations.}
The current BC policy does not generalize to unseen anatomical
configurations significantly different from the training phantom.
Sim-to-real transfer via a PyBullet bronchial simulator with
PPO fine-tuning is planned as future work.
The 20\,Hz loop is bottlenecked by Depth-Anything-V2; a
lighter depth encoder (e.g., ViT-S) could free headroom for
higher control frequencies.

% ── IX. Conclusion ──────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}

We presented an integrated embodied bronchoscope platform combining
multimodal perception with goal-conditioned imitation learning.
The 20-dimensional structured observation fuses UNet segmentation,
Depth-Anything-V2 metric depth, and DepthPathFinder path analysis
with motor proprioception.
BronchusPolicy, a GRU network with a goal-embedding mechanism and a
dual-head training objective, achieves \TODO{XX\%} navigation success
at 20\,Hz on a bronchial phantom.
Future work will extend the framework to PPO-based reinforcement
learning in simulation and validate sim-to-real transfer on ex-vivo
porcine tissue.
